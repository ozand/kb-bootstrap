# JTBD and customer journey map

Read when the brief asks why users need the product area and how they move through it.

## Jobs to be done (3–7 per study)

Format — situation, motivation, outcome, with evidence:

```markdown
| # | When… | I want to… | So that… | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | I finish a client call | get the decisions and tasks without re-listening | I can send a follow-up within an hour | [raw 031](…) review quote; [raw 008](…) template gallery |
```

- A job describes the user's progress, not a feature ("find what was agreed" rather than
  "use search").
- Every job needs at least one captured source (review, case study, forum post, template
  description). Jobs inferred only from your reasoning go under "Hypotheses".
- If the project has its own JTBD/UX audit, read it first and map market jobs to it.

## Customer journey map (for the studied area)

```markdown
| Stage | User actions | Pain points (evidence) | Competitor solutions | Our product today | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discover a past meeting | scrolls the list, types a name | names are raw file names ([raw 012](…)) | auto titles, search in transcripts (Product A UI) | only 100 items visible | full-archive search |
```

- 4–7 stages from trigger to outcome; include what happens *after* the main action
  (sharing, follow-up, cleanup).
- Pain points need evidence; opportunities are your conclusions — mark them as such.
- The "our product today" column is checked against the product's code or UI, not assumed.
